Why Do We Keep Forgetting the Same Words When Learning Languages?

Why do we keep forgetting foreign words? Discover practical ways to improve vocabulary retention, review difficult words, and learn multiple languages more effectively.

ParallelLingvo Team
·Oct 14, 2026·3 min read

Why are some foreign words easy to remember while others seem impossible to learn? Almost everyone studying a new language has experienced this problem. And when you're learning two or three languages simultaneously, remembering vocabulary can become even more challenging.

But the problem isn't necessarily a poor memory. Often, it's the way we learn and review vocabulary.

Recognizing a Word Isn't the Same as Remembering It

Imagine reading something in a foreign language and immediately recognizing a familiar word. Later, during a conversation, you try to use that same word but can't remember it.

This happens because recognizing information and retrieving it from memory are different skills.

When reading, the word itself provides a clue. During a conversation, your brain needs to retrieve the word without that assistance.

Simply looking through vocabulary lists isn't always enough. Actively recalling words and using them in different situations helps strengthen your ability to remember them.

Why Learning Multiple Languages Can Be Challenging

Imagine studying English, Dutch, and German at the same time.

Some words look or sound similar, making them easier to remember. However, these similarities can also cause confusion.

Managing separate vocabulary lists for every language pair creates another challenge. You may find yourself switching between different dictionaries and repeating the same organizational work.

A more convenient approach is to connect translations across languages through a single vocabulary concept.

This is one of the ideas behind ParallelLingvo.

Focus on Words You Actually Find Difficult

Not every word requires the same amount of practice.

Some words become familiar almost immediately. Others remain difficult even after repeated exposure.

Instead of reviewing everything equally, identify the vocabulary that needs extra attention.

Mark difficult words, return to them regularly, practice their pronunciation, and try using them in your own sentences.

You can also challenge yourself to remember a translation before checking the answer.

The goal isn't simply to spend more time studying. It's to make your practice more focused and useful.

Learning More Words Isn't Always the Answer

Language learning isn't a competition to memorize the largest possible number of words.

You might study dozens of new words every day, but without revisiting previous vocabulary, much of that knowledge can gradually fade.

A more sustainable approach combines learning new vocabulary with reviewing words that still need practice.
