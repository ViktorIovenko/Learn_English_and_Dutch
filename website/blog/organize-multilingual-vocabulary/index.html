MULTILINGUAL STUDY METHOD

How to organize multilingual vocabulary without confusion

September 6, 2026 · 4 min read

When you study Dutch, English and Russian together, vocabulary management can become harder than the vocabulary itself. Organize by concept and lesson instead of by language.

Use one vocabulary item for one concept

Let one item represent one meaning and attach the Dutch, English and Russian versions to it. If a word has several unrelated meanings, create separate items.

Organize by lessons, not by language

Instead of folders called Dutch, English and Russian, use lessons such as Work, Housing, School or a chapter number. The languages become properties of the same item.

Choose a primary language for each session

Having three translations does not mean you need to test all directions at once. Pick the language you want to recall and use the other two as support.

Keep examples close to ambiguous words

Add a short example when context, grammar or usage differs between the three languages.

Separate difficult vocabulary from ordinary review

Mark difficult words and repeat them more often instead of treating familiar and weak items equally.

Simple structure: Lesson → concept → NL/EN/RU → example or audio → learning status.
