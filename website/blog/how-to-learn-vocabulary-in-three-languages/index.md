# How to learn vocabulary in three languages at the same time

Published: 2026-09-08

Learning vocabulary in three languages at once becomes easier when every language pair is not treated as a separate project. A more efficient method is to connect several translations to one core concept and practise that concept as a multilingual unit.

## Start with one concept, not three separate lists

Suppose you are learning Dutch `ontwikkeling`. A bilingual list may connect it only with English `development`. If Russian is also part of your learning, you may create another list containing `ontwikkeling — развитие`. The same concept is now duplicated.

A multilingual structure keeps the meanings together: Dutch `ontwikkeling`, English `development`, Russian `развитие`, and additional translations when needed.

## Use one main learning direction

Several translations do not mean every session must test every direction. Choose one primary prompt language for the session and use the other translations as support. Reverse the direction in another session.

## Keep lessons small and meaningful

Divide vocabulary into lessons based on a topic, course chapter, exam section or practical context. Small lessons are easier to repeat than one huge multilingual list.

- Use roughly 10–30 related words in one lesson.
- Keep several translations attached to each word.
- Mark difficult vocabulary instead of repeating everything equally.
- Use pronunciation audio when the spoken form matters.

## Compare languages without translating mechanically

Translations are connected meanings, not always identical words. Context, grammar and usage can differ. Example sentences help when a direct translation is ambiguous.

## Repeat difficult words more often

Review difficult items frequently while allowing familiar vocabulary to appear less often. This is more efficient than restarting the whole lesson.

## Why LearnWord uses this structure

LearnWord is designed around lessons, vocabulary items and multiple translations. The goal is to give multilingual learners one place where a concept can remain connected across three or more languages, with pronunciation and learning progress attached.

## Summary

Keep one concept at the center, attach several translations, practise one main direction at a time, divide vocabulary into small lessons and focus repetition on difficult words.
